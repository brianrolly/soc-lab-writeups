Catching Discovery on the Endpoint

Adversary emulation with CALDERA, endpoint telemetry from auditd, and detection in Wazuh — emulate the discovery an intruder runs in their first minutes on a host, then prove every command raises a MITRE-mapped alert.

Emulation CALDERA · Sandcat Telemetry Linux auditd 9 rules · 8 techniques

Overview

Perimeter and SIEM detections watch traffic; they do not watch what an intruder types once they are on a host. This is the endpoint layer — emulating an attacker’s first-minutes reconnaissance with CALDERA, and proving Linux auditd and Wazuh turn each command into a MITRE-mapped alert.

The emulation

What the agent actually ran

A CALDERA Sandcat agent executed a realistic discovery sequence — the reconnaissance an attacker runs to understand where they’ve landed. The agent binary itself was planted as /root/splunkd, masquerading as a legitimate service (T1036).

Takeaway

Endpoint discovery is often where an intrusion first becomes visible, and it is low-signal by nature — a single whoami is nothing; the pattern is everything. Mapping each behaviour to ATT&CK turns a pile of exec events into coverage a SOC can report on, and reason about the gaps in.

Authorized lab. CALDERA adversary emulation against the author’s own host. MITRE ATT&CK: Discovery (TA0007) — T1033, T1087.001, T1057, T1069.001, T1082, T1016, plus T1036 and T1518.
