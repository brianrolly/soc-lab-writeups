The Query That Exposed a Broken SIEM

Asking the SIEM a week-long question failed with a circuit-breaker error — and uncovered a capacity defect that had no symptom until something asked a bigger question. It would have silently broken every dashboard to come.

Symptom circuit_breaking_exception Root cause 1 GB heap on a 12 GB host Fixed · verified

Overview

This began as routine measurement — a week-long query to tune a detection threshold — and became a capacity finding. The most dangerous defects in a SIEM are the ones with no symptom, and this was one of them.

The symptom

Day one worked; day seven fell over

opensearch7-day aggregation
circuit_breaking_exception: [parent] Data too large … 974.2mb,
which is larger than the limit of 972.7mb
  request=0b   fielddata=102.9kb   in_flight=1.1kb
tellThe query itself used almost nothing (request=0b). The heap was already full before the query arrived.

The diagnosis

Undersized, not failing

Heap

1 GB configured on a host with 12 GB total and ~9 GB free and idle.

Shards

540 — roughly 27× the ~20-per-GB rule of thumb. Standing shard overhead ate ~850 MB before any query.

GC

0 old-gen collections — not thrashing, just starved. Caught before degradation.

~150 MB of query scratch was left. One day of data fit; seven did not. The circuit breaker did its job — it rejected the query instead of crashing, which is exactly why there had been no outage and no symptom.

The fix

4 GB, verified, no downtime cost

jvm.optionsafter restart
-Xms1g -Xmx1g   →   -Xms4g -Xmx4g
heap: 4096MB max, 27% used at rest
7-day query: completes, all days, no errors
noteChose 4 GB, not 50%-of-RAM: manager, dashboard and filebeat share the host, and OS page cache is what makes searches fast. Don’t starve it.

Why it mattered

A silent blocker, cleared early

It was invisible

Nothing had asked the cluster a question spanning more than a day. The failure mode would have been a blank dashboard panel with no obvious cause.

It would have spread

Every multi-day dashboard, every telemetry pilot, every "has this source been here before?" hunt would have hit the same wall.

Root cause remains

The heap raise bought headroom; 540 shards is the underlying defect. The real fix is an ISM rollover policy — tracked, not urgent.

Operational note recorded for the team: on this single-node cluster, yellow with unassigned replicas is the correct steady state, and red for the first minute after a restart is recovery, not failure — a distinction that had already caused one needless rollback.

Takeaway

The fix was one line; the value was catching it before it mattered. A monitoring platform that silently cannot answer multi-day questions is worse than one that is obviously down — at least the latter gets fixed. The investigation also produced the operational facts the team now relies on: what healthy looks like on a single-node cluster, and what is merely recovery.

Authorized lab. Capacity finding on the author’s own single-node Wazuh/OpenSearch cluster, found while measuring detection data.
